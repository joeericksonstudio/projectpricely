Roof Replacement prices in Florida

Florida pricing is close to the national average, with Miami and South Florida running higher than the Panhandle and central regions. Hurricane-season demand spikes can tighten contractor availability. Our estimate applies a regional adjustment of 0.97x the national average rate. Treat this as a planning figure - metro areas inside Florida can sit well above or below the state-level adjustment, and only written quotes from licensed local contractors settle a real price.

What drives the price

  • Roofing material (asphalt is cheapest; metal, tile, and slate multiply cost)
  • Roof size - roof area is about 1.1x the floor area of a single-story home with a standard 6:12 pitch
  • Pitch and complexity (steep slopes, valleys, dormers slow the crew)
  • Tear-off vs overlay (removing old layers adds $1 to $3 per sq ft)
  • Decking repairs discovered after tear-off
  • Skylights, chimneys, and flashing details

What to know before you get quotes

Roofing is priced per square, meaning 100 square feet of roof area, with asphalt shingle replacement running $300 to $600 per square installed. Labor is half or more of the total at $150 to $300 per square, which is why regional labor markets move quotes so much. Roof area is not floor area: a single-story home with a standard pitch has roughly 1.1 times its floor area in roof, and steep pitches add more. The material decision is the big lever - asphalt architectural shingles are the budget standard, standing-seam metal roughly doubles the price but lasts 40 to 70 years, and tile or slate can triple it while outlasting the house. Quotes should specify tear-off versus overlay (most codes allow only two shingle layers, and tear-off adds $1 to $3 per square foot), the underlayment type, ice-and-water shield coverage, flashing replacement, and ventilation work. The unpleasant surprise in roofing is decking: rotted sheathing found after tear-off typically adds $50 to $100 per sheet replaced. Get at least three itemized bids and confirm each includes disposal, permits, and a workmanship warranty.

FAQ

How often should a roof be replaced?

Standard asphalt shingles last 20 to 30 years, architectural shingles 25 to 35, metal 40 to 70, and tile or slate 50 to 100+. Granule loss, curling, and repeated leaks are the replacement signals.

Can I roof over my existing shingles?

Most codes permit one overlay layer, saving $1 to $3 per square foot on tear-off, but it hides decking damage, adds weight, and usually voids the full manufacturer warranty.

Does homeowners insurance pay for roof replacement?

Insurance typically covers sudden damage (wind, hail, fallen trees) but not wear and age. Document storm damage promptly and get a contractor's inspection report before filing.
