Fence Installation prices in Tennessee

Tennessee labor costs sit a touch below the national average, with Nashville's building boom pulling Middle Tennessee toward national pricing while Memphis and Knoxville stay cheaper. No state income tax on wages and steady in-migration keep contractor pipelines full, and spring storm season drives reliable demand for roofing and exterior work. Our estimate applies a regional adjustment of 0.97x the national average rate. Treat this as a planning figure - metro areas inside Tennessee can sit well above or below the state-level adjustment, and only written quotes from licensed local contractors settle a real price.

What drives the price

  • Material (chain-link is cheapest; wrought iron and composite are premium)
  • Fence height (each additional 2 feet adds materially to cost)
  • Terrain and slope (grading and stepped panels add labor)
  • Number and width of gates
  • Old fence removal and disposal ($10 to $20 per linear foot)
  • Post depth and soil conditions (rocky soil slows setting)

What to know before you get quotes

Fencing is priced per linear foot installed, and the material choice drives the price more than any other factor: chain-link starts around $10 to $40 per foot while wrought iron runs $50 to $85. Wood privacy fencing, the most common residential choice, sits in the $20 to $50 range before regional labor differences. Height is the second lever - moving from a 4-foot to a 6-foot fence adds roughly a third to material and labor, and 8-foot privacy fences cost $50 to $85 per foot because posts, panels, and wind bracing all scale up. Gates are quoted separately in most bids, typically $150 to $600 each depending on width and hardware. Budget items homeowners forget: old fence removal ($10 to $20 per foot), grading on sloped lots, and permits, which many municipalities require for fences over 6 feet or along front property lines. Corner lots and rocky soil push labor up. Get quotes that specify post spacing, post depth, concrete per post, and panel material grade - two bids at the same per-foot price can describe very different fences.

FAQ

What is the cheapest fence to install?

Barbed wire ($3 to $6 per linear foot) and chain-link ($10 to $40 per linear foot) are the cheapest options. For privacy, basic pine or spruce wood fencing ($20 to $50 per linear foot) is the budget pick.

Does a fence add value to a home?

A well-built privacy fence typically returns 50% to 70% of its cost at resale, and it can speed up a sale for buyers with dogs or children.

Do I need a permit to build a fence?

Most cities require permits for fences over 6 feet tall, and many regulate front-yard fence height and materials. Check your local planning department before posts go in.
